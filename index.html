MOVEit Third-Party Breach (2023-2024)

  • Incident: Third-party vendor MOVEit file transfer software compromised
  • Scale: 2.8 million employee records exposed
  • Vulnerability: SQL injection in MOVEit software exploited by Cl0p ransomware group
  • Data Exposed: Work contact information (names, emails, phone numbers, building locations)
  • Amazon's Response: Immediate vendor isolation, clarified AWS systems uncompromised
  • Legal Implications: Highlights vendor risk under DPDP Act
4 / 31

Amazon India Seller Data Breach (January 2019)

  • Incident: Technical glitch exposed financial data of sellers
  • Data Exposed: Sales volumes, inventory data, category splits, merchant tax reports
  • Affected: 400,000+ sellers on platform
  • Resolution: Fixed within hours, minimal disclosure
  • Legal Framework at Time: IT Act 2000 only - limited enforcement
  • Significance: Exposed competitive business intelligence of sellers
5 / 31

Individual Customer Data Compromise (2024)

  • Incident: Order duplication and counterfeit product delivery
  • Suggests: Order information leaked and exploited by malicious actors
  • Concern: Third-party seller access vulnerabilities
  • Investigation: Ongoing, no conclusive findings released
  • Pattern: Highlights persistent third-party ecosystem risks

India's Cybersecurity Legal Framework

  • Three Main Pillars:
    • Information Technology Act, 2000
    • Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
    • Sector-Specific Regulations (RBI, SEBI)
  • Supporting Framework:
    • Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020
    • Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code, 2021
7 / 31

IT Act 2000: Foundation of Cyber Law

  • Section 43: Civil remedies for unauthorized access to computer systems
  • Section 66: Hacking - 3 years imprisonment + ₹5 lakh fine
  • Section 72: Breach of confidentiality - 2 years + ₹1 lakh fine
  • Section 72A: Service provider breaches - 3 years + ₹5 lakh fine
  • Focus: Criminal and civil liability for cyber offenses
  • Limitation: Penalties insufficient for modern data breaches
8 / 31

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

  • Enacted: August 2023 - Game changer for data protection
  • Introduces: Comprehensive privacy framework comparable to GDPR
  • Key Concepts:
    • Data Fiduciaries (entities processing personal data)
    • Mandatory breach notification within 72 hours
    • Massive penalties up to ₹250 crore
    • Data Protection Board of India (enforcement authority)
9 / 31

Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) Requirements

  • Amazon.in likely designated as SDF based on:
    • Large volume of personal data processed
    • Sensitive payment and financial information
    • Millions of users across India
  • Enhanced Obligations:
    • Appoint Data Protection Officer in India
    • Annual Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)
    • Independent data audits
    • Cross-border transfer restrictions
    • 3-year data retention limit for e-commerce
10 / 31

Amazon's Multi-Layered Security Infrastructure

  • Data Encryption: AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.2+ in transit
  • Two-Factor Authentication: Mandatory for all seller accounts since March 2024
  • Access Controls: Least privilege principle implementation
  • Threat Detection: Amazon GuardDuty with ML-based anomaly detection
  • Fraud Detection: ML-based Amazon Fraud Detector
  • Compliance: PCI DSS certified for payment processing
11 / 31

Encryption Protocols

  • At Rest: AES-256 encryption for all S3 buckets storing customer data
  • In Transit: TLS 1.2 or higher (HTTPS) for all data transmission
  • Key Management: AWS Key Management Service (KMS) for customer-managed keys
  • Hardware-level: XTS-AES-256 for EC2 instance stores
  • Coverage: All data states throughout entire lifecycle
12 / 31

Access Control Mechanisms

  • Mandatory 2FA: Required for all seller accounts since March 2024
  • Unique Credentials: No shared logins permitted across accounts
  • Least Privilege: Access limited strictly to required tasks only
  • Automated Monitoring: Failed login detection, suspicious activity alerts
  • Immediate Revocation: Access terminated upon employee departure
  • Audit Logs: Comprehensive logging of all access events
13 / 31

Amazon GuardDuty: AI-Powered Threat Detection

  • Service Type: Fully managed threat detection service
  • Scale: Monitors billions of events across AWS infrastructure
  • Technology: Machine learning-based anomaly detection
  • Intelligence: Integrates global threat intelligence feeds
  • Response: Automated response workflows for identified threats
  • Performance: Detection time reduced from hours to <1 minute
14 / 31

Third-Party Vendor Security Requirements

  • Comprehensive vendor policy requirements:
    • Annual penetration testing mandatory
    • Security certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2)
    • Firewall configurations with annual reviews
    • Documented incident response procedures
  • Amazon Audit Rights: Reserved in all vendor contracts
  • Contractual Accountability: Breach liability clauses
  • Gap Revealed: MOVEit breach exposed vendor oversight deficiencies
15 / 31

2019 Seller Breach: Then vs. Now

Under 2019 Legal Framework:
  • Limited IT Act penalties (max ₹5 lakh)
  • Section 72 difficult to apply (technical glitch vs. willful disclosure)
  • Minimal regulatory exposure and enforcement
Under Current DPDP Act:
  • ₹250 crore: Failure to implement adequate security safeguards
  • ₹200 crore: Failure to notify within 72 hours
  • Total exposure: ₹450 crore+ for identical incident
16 / 31

MOVEit Breach Under DPDP Framework

  • Mandatory breach notification to:
    • Data Protection Board of India within 72 hours
    • All affected individuals without undue delay
  • Vendor Due Diligence Failure:
    • SDF must ensure vendor security measures adequate
    • Failure to monitor vendor patching = regulatory penalty
  • No Safe Harbor: Liability exists regardless of third-party origin
17 / 31

Data Residency & Localization

  • RBI Mandate (2018): Payment data stored exclusively in India
  • SEBI Mandate (2023): Cloud data storage within India for regulated entities
  • DPDP Act: SDFs cannot transfer certain data categories abroad without consent
  • Impact on Amazon:
    • Requires dedicated Indian data center infrastructure
    • Conflicts with global AWS operational model
    • Substantial capital investment needed
18 / 31

Amazon.in: Critical Compliance Gaps

  • No Data Protection Officer appointed in India
  • Data Retention: Practices exceed 3-year e-commerce limit
  • Global Infrastructure: Conflicts with localization requirements
  • Vendor Security Oversight: Insufficient as demonstrated by MOVEit breach
  • Breach Notification: Procedures inadequate for 72-hour timeline
  • Annual DPIA and Audits: Not yet implemented for SDF compliance
19 / 31

Hypothetical Breach: Total Legal Exposure

Scenario: 10 million customers' financial data breach
  • DPDP Act Penalties: ₹450 crore (security + notification failure)
  • IT Act: Criminal prosecution under Sections 66, 72A
  • Consumer Protection: Fall-back liability + individual compensation
  • RBI Enforcement: Payment data violation penalties
  • Total Estimated Exposure: ₹1,000+ crore
  • Plus: Operational restrictions during investigation period
20 / 31

E-Commerce Rules & Platform Accountability

  • Framework: Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020
  • Grievance Redressal: 48-hour acknowledgment, 1-month resolution requirement
  • Fall-back Liability: Platforms directly responsible for seller actions
  • National Consumer Commission: Precedent - Amazon cannot disclaim responsibility
  • Extension: Liability extends to cybersecurity failures enabling fraud
21 / 31

Multi-Channel Complaint System

  • National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCCRP): For data breaches and cyber fraud
  • Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions: Platform accountability claims
  • Future Data Protection Board: DPDP Act violation enforcement
  • Intermediary Grievance Officers: 24-hour acknowledgment requirement
  • Result: Multiple accountability pathways for affected customers
22 / 31

DPDP Act Implementation Status

  • August 2023: DPDP Act enacted by Parliament
  • January 2025: Draft DPDP Rules released for consultation
  • 2025: Enforcement-heavy phase expected to begin
  • Gap: Data Protection Board of India not yet established
  • Uncertainty: Final rules still under consultation process
  • Industry Reaction: Amazon, Google, Meta requesting implementation delays
23 / 31

Compliance Roadmap: Urgent Actions Required

Immediate Actions:
  • Appoint Data Protection Officer in India
  • Establish 72-hour breach notification procedures
  • Conduct comprehensive data audit and inventory
Medium-term (6-12 months):
  • Build dedicated Indian data residency infrastructure
  • Implement 3-year data retention policies
  • Enhance vendor security auditing framework
Long-term:
  • Annual DPIAs and independent security audits
  • Cross-border transfer controls and consent mechanisms
24 / 31

India's Data Protection Trajectory

  • Trend: Comprehensive framework comparable to EU's GDPR
  • Focus: Data sovereignty and mandatory localization
  • Enforcement Phase: Transitioning from enactment to active enforcement
  • Message: Multinational platforms must restructure data practices
  • Reality: Regulatory inevitability overrides operational preferences
  • Adaptation Required: Compliance is no longer optional
25 / 31

Key Takeaways

  • Amazon operates in increasingly rigorous legal framework
  • Past breaches would trigger massive penalties under current law
  • Significant compliance gaps require urgent remediation
  • Defense in depth, zero trust, proactive detection essential
  • Third-party risk management critical - MOVEit lessons
  • Transparency and rapid incident response mandatory
  • Legal exposure exceeds ₹1,000 crore for major breach

Conclusion: Regulatory Transformation Underway

  • India constructing comprehensive data protection infrastructure
  • DPDP Act represents watershed moment in privacy regulation
  • Amazon.in faces substantial legal exposure without full compliance
  • Proactive investment in compliance required now
  • Enforcement will intensify as Data Protection Board activates
  • Message: Adapt to regulatory transformation or face severe consequences
